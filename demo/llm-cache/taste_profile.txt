# Your Taste Profile

## The big picture
You like smart, tightly built stories with moral weight, delivered with craft and a dry or dark sense of humor. You're drawn to institutions and systems (spy agencies, police forces, corporations, empires) and to the flawed people trapped inside them. You tolerate slow burns well but want payoff, and you're equally happy with a crackling comedy or a grim procedural. You watch widely across countries, and a strong British and Indian streak runs through your history.

---

## Taste clusters (largest first)

### 1. Prestige crime, noir & moral descent (~21 titles)
This is your core. You like methodical, character-driven crime stories where the investigation or the rise to power corrodes everyone involved. *Breaking Bad*, *Better Call Saul*, *Ozark*, *The Wire*, *True Detective*, *Mindhunter*, *Zodiac*, *Se7en*, *Gone Girl*, *The Social Network*, *The Godfather*, *Goodfellas*, *Pulp Fiction*, *The Dark Knight*, *The Shawshank Redemption*, *Spotlight*, *Squid Game*, *Succession*, *Mad Max: Fury Road* (the lone action outlier here, but it's a story of resisting tyranny), *Sacred Games*, and *Delhi Crime*. Fincher shows up repeatedly, so you respond to cold control and procedural obsession. You want ambiguity, not catharsis.

### 2. British & UK-set drama, comedy and crime (~15 titles)
Britain runs through many of your genres, so it gets its own cluster. *Slow Horses*, *Fleabag*, *Broadchurch*, *Line of Duty*, *Happy Valley*, *Bodyguard*, *The Night Manager*, *Sherlock*, *Downton Abbey*, *Queen*, *Hot Fuzz*, *Paddington 2*, *Grand Designs*, *Great British Bake Off*, and *Last Week Tonight with John Oliver* (British wit, American subject matter). You like dry, mordant humor, institutional paranoia, grey weather, and procedural rigor, but you also have a soft spot for cozy, gentle British warmth. *Bake Off*, *Grand Designs*, and *Paddington 2* show a comfort-viewing side that sits right next to the grim stuff.

### 3. Espionage & high-concept thrillers (~11 titles)
Spycraft, conspiracy, and puzzle-box plotting. *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy*, *Andor*, *Inception*, *The Bourne Identity*, *Mission: Impossible - Fallout*, *Skyfall*, *Casino Royale* (1967), *Top Gun: Maverick*, *The Matrix*, *Knives Out*, and *Only Murders in the Building*. You like the slow, paranoid Le Carré end and the propulsive blockbuster end, plus witty whodunits. Gary Oldman, Nolan-style structure, and "who can you trust?" tension recur.

### 4. Cerebral & epic sci-fi / fantasy (~11 titles)
Big ideas, serious tone, deliberate pacing. *Interstellar*, *Arrival*, *Dune*, *Blade Runner 2049*, *The Expanse*, *Dark*, *Severance*, *Shogun* (epic scale, political intrigue), *The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring*, *Oppenheimer*, and *Jurassic Park*. Villeneuve and Nolan are clear favorites. You like worldbuilding grounded in plausible rules, existential stakes, and melancholy over spectacle.

### 5. Hindi & Indian cinema/series (~9 titles)
Another country that crosses genres. *Panchayat* (gentle village satire), *Andhadhun* (dark comic thriller), *3 Idiots* and *Dangal* (crowd-pleasing, socially minded drama), *Zindagi Na Milegi Dobara* (friendship road-trip), *The Family Man* (thriller), *RRR* (Telugu action spectacle), plus *Sacred Games* and *Delhi Crime* (counted above under crime). Your range here is wide: you move easily from rural comedy to gritty crime to uplifting sports drama.

### 6. Warm, character-driven comedy (~9 titles)
Humane, ensemble-based, optimistic. *Ted Lasso*, *Parks and Recreation*, *The Office*, *Schitt's Creek*, *The Bear* (comedic energy, but with real intensity), *Back to the Future*, *La La Land*, *Everything Everywhere All at Once*, and *Stranger Things*. You like earnest comedy with heart, where people grow and found families form.

### 7. Animation & family-friendly wonder (~4 titles)
*Spirited Away*, *Coco*, *Up*, and *Toy Story*. You respond to animation that's emotionally rich and often bittersweet. These aren't just kids' films for you; grief, memory, and growing up are the draws.

### 8. Real-world documentary & history (~5 titles)
*Planet Earth*, *Our Planet*, *Chernobyl*, *Band of Brothers*, and *The Last of Us* (grounded, human-scale apocalypse drama that sits closer to prestige TV, but fits the "authentic, unglamorous survival" thread). You want immersion, authenticity, and moral seriousness. Attenborough's narration is a clear comfort.

### 9. Stylized & international one-offs (~4 titles)
*Parasite* (Korean), *The Grand Budapest Hotel*, *Scam 1992* (Hindi, counted here for its Indian setting... see note below), and *Squid Game* are covered elsewhere. This slot holds the oddities that don't fit a larger group: Wes Anderson's whimsy and Bong Joon-ho's class satire show you'll follow a distinctive auteur voice anywhere.

*(Note: counts above are approximate groupings; each title appears in exactly one cluster.)*

## What you keep coming back to
- **Institutions as characters.** MI5, the LAPD-style bureaucracy, Lumon, the Empire, the Church. You love stories about how systems shape and break people.
- **Moral ambiguity.** Nearly nobody in your history is purely good. Descent, complicity, and compromise recur.
- **Directors with a signature.** Fincher, Nolan, Villeneuve, Scorsese, Bong Joon-ho, Wes Anderson.
- **Deliberate pacing with a payoff.** You'll invest in slow builds, but you also enjoy brisk, funny, propulsive pieces.
- **Dry wit.** Even your darkest picks usually carry some gallows humor.

## Notable patterns
- **A dual personality.** Grim, cynical crime and espionage sit alongside cozy, wholesome comfort viewing. Both are real; you toggle between them.
- **Genuine international range.** British, Hindi/Indian, Korean, German, Japanese, and Russian titles appear, mostly consumed without any "foreign film" hesitation.
- **Strong ensemble preference.** You like casts where everyone has a stake, from the *Slow Horses* misfits to the Roy siblings.
- **Little interest in pure spectacle or horror.** Even your action and sci-fi picks are character- or idea-led.

## What you tend to skip
You asked for less of *Money Heist*, *The Crown*, and *Black Mirror*. Reading across them, the pattern is:
- **Glossy, high-concept heist or caper thrills** where the plot's cleverness outweighs the characters (*Money Heist*).
- **Lavish, prestige-royal or period costume drama played reverently** (*The Crown*), even though you've enjoyed lighter or more satirical takes on British institutions.
- **Anthology-style, twist-of-the-week techno-dystopia** (*Black Mirror*), where the idea is the star and the characters are disposable.

In short, you lean away from showy, self-consciously "big" concepts and slow ceremonial grandeur, in favor of grounded, character-first storytelling with real stakes.
