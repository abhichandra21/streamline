# Casino Royale (1967)

This delightfully chaotic spy spoof stampedes through swinging '60s psychedelia with a deliberately fractured pace, deploying an all-star ensemble cast in a madcap British comedy that gleefully dismantles spy-fi conventions through absurdist humor and visual pandemonium. The film's anarchic tone—driven by Peter Sellers' stammering bumbling and Orson Welles' scenery-chewing villainy—prioritizes comedic confusion and surreal set pieces over coherent plotting, capturing the era's irreverent rejection of serious espionage narratives with kaleidoscopic production design and deadpan British wit. Thematically preoccupied with intelligence incompetence and the ridiculousness of Cold War spy culture, it's a rambling, ego-driven romp that feels less like a narrative and more like a series of comedic riffs on baccarat tables, secret agents, and royal intrigue.
