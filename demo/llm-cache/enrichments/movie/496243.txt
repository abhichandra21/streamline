# Parasite

Bong Joon-ho's darkly comedic thriller executes a taut, escalating con with breakneck pacing that pivots from sly humor to genuine dread, as an unemployed Seoul family's schemes to infiltrate a wealthy household meticulously unpeel the absurdities and resentments simmering beneath South Korea's rigid class hierarchy. The film's tone oscillates between sitcom-like scheming and suffocating psychological tension, grounding its biting social commentary in the specificity of Korean domestic spaces—semi-basements and modernist mansions—where every architectural detail becomes a weapon of class humiliation. What emerges is a tragicomic examination of systemic exploitation and desperation that refuses easy moral judgments, instead trapping viewers in a darkening spiral where no one's hands stay clean and the film's final act upends genre expectations entirely.
