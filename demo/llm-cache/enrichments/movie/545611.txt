# Everything Everywhere All at Once

A kinetic, wildly surreal odyssey that barrels through infinite parallel universes at breakneck speed, anchored by Michelle Yeoh's exhausted Chinese immigrant mother navigating both laundromat taxes and existential nihilism with unexpected grace. The film swings violently between intimate family trauma and absurdist martial arts spectacle, blending kung fu balletics with indie-film weirdness while grappling with generational rifts, parental disappointment, and whether meaning exists at all. Its frenetic, maximalist sensibility—layering sight gags, philosophical dread, and genuine tenderness—captures the disorienting chaos of immigrant life and mother-daughter complexity through a lens that feels simultaneously deeply personal and cosmically unhinged.
